Reviewer 1

The manuscript proposes a multi-modal fusion framework combining CNN-BiLSTM, backbone/sidechain structural descriptors, and MolFormer embeddings, with a well-motivated design and sound ablation studies that provide physically interpretable property-modality mappings. However, the following issues need to be addressed:

1. There is a serious discrepancy between Table 1 and Table 2, and the comparison lacks recent SOTA baselines. Table 1 (Section 2.1) reports the full model's five-fold cross-validation MAE as Tg=53.39K, Tc=0.02603, FFV=0.00472, ρ=0.02576, and Rg=1.322Å. However, Table 2 (Section 2.4) reports drastically different values for the same "Poly-Prophet (this work)" model: Tg=4.02K, Tc=0.0034, FFV=0.0022, ρ=0.0041, and Rg=0.225Å — roughly an order-of-magnitude improvement across all five properties, with a Tg MAE of 4.02K far surpassing any previously published method, which is physically implausible. This likely stems from an inconsistent evaluation protocol, data leakage, or a unit/scaling error introduced during the inverse target-scaling procedure described in Section 4.2.2. This is the most serious issue in the manuscript and must be clarified and corrected, or the "state-of-the-art" claim cannot be trusted. In addition, the baselines currently cited in Table 2 (TransTg, LSTM, Multimodal Transformer, Pareto-SMILES, Bioplastic DNN, Polymer-unit Graph) are not the strongest or most representative pretrained models in this field. The authors should include comparisons with more recent, widely-used polymer-specific pretrained language models such as polyBERT and PolyNC to make the SOTA claim credible.

2. The literature comparison in Table 2 lacks a unified evaluation protocol. The cited baseline numbers are taken directly from their original papers, which likely used different data splits and preprocessing pipelines (the manuscript itself acknowledges that fair comparison "necessitates evaluating models on identical or highly similar data distributions," yet provides no evidence that this condition is met). The authors should either re-run these baselines (including the newly requested polyBERT and PolyNC) under the same data split and evaluation protocol as their own model, or reframe Table 2 as a qualitative literature summary rather than a table implying quantitative superiority.

3. Variance and statistical significance are not reported. All results are presented as single-point MAE estimates with no standard deviation or confidence interval across folds, yet several ablation conclusions rely on very small differences (e.g., FFV changes by only 0.00024 when removing CNN; density MAE for "w/o CNN" is actually 0.00034 g/cm³ better than the full model). The authors should report mean ± standard deviation across folds for all metrics and apply a paired statistical test (e.g., paired t-test) for key ablation comparisons, otherwise it is unclear whether these differences are meaningful or simply fold-to-fold noise.

Reviewer 2

I reviewed the manuscript carefully. The main issues are related to data leakage/generalization, benchmark consistency, validation methodology, representation limitations, and the interpretation of the ablation results. Here are the comments:
1. The manuscript should provide a much clearer description of the dataset composition, including the total number of polymers, the number of samples available for each target property, and the extent of overlap among the primary and supplementary datasets, because these factors strongly affect the reliability of the reported multi-property predictions.
2. The use of five-fold cross-validation requires further clarification regarding data leakage, particularly because duplicate or structurally related SMILES representations are present and SMILES augmentation is applied; the authors should demonstrate that different representations of the same polymer or highly similar polymers cannot appear across both training and validation folds.
3. There is a substantial inconsistency between the predictive errors reported in Table 1 and those reported in Table 2, where the proposed model is stated to achieve a \(T_g\) MAE of 53.39 K in the cross-validation results but 4.02 K in the literature comparison; the authors must explain whether these values originate from different datasets, splits, evaluation protocols, or preprocessing procedures.
4. The claim that Poly-Prophet establishes a “new state-of-the-art” should be reconsidered unless all compared models are evaluated under the same dataset, target definition, data split, and evaluation metric, since direct comparison with results obtained from different datasets or experimental conditions may lead to an unfair assessment of relative performance.
5. The manuscript would benefit from a more quantitative uncertainty analysis, such as confidence intervals, fold-to-fold variability, or prediction intervals, because reporting only mean MAE values does not sufficiently establish the robustness of the model, particularly for polymer properties with potentially heterogeneous data distributions.
Although the authors acknowledge that MolFormer was pretrained primarily on small molecules, the manuscript should investigate this domain mismatch more rigorously by comparing the pretrained representation with a polymer-specific representation or by conducting additional transfer-learning experiments to determine whether the observed improvement is genuinely meaningful for polymer property prediction.
6. The reliance on repeat-unit SMILES and handcrafted backbone/side-chain descriptors limits the physical information available to the model, particularly for properties affected by molecular weight, chain conformation, crystallinity, entanglement, and three-dimensional packing; therefore, the authors should discuss more explicitly how these omitted variables may influence the reported prediction errors and the practical applicability of the proposed framework.
7. The following articles could be used for the litearture and other sections to support the findings: A General Strategy for Exceptionally Robust Conducting Polymer-Based Bioelectrodes with Multimodal Capabilities Through Decoupled Charge Transport Mechanisms; Study on Creep Mechanical Properties of HTPB Solid Propellant; Meso-structure study of recycled aggregate stabilized by multi-source solid waste geopolymer based on discrete element method
